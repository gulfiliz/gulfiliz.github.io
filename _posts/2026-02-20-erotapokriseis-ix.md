**1) Q:** [Bell's Theorem]({% post_url 2026-01-12-bells-theorem %}) forces you to give up one of three things: locality, realism, or free will. Which one do you let go of, and which one do you refuse to?

**A:** I refuse to give up free will; if something has to die, let it be the innocent little classical realism that thought the world was just sitting there with answers in its pockets. The world can be strange. I can live with strange. :)
What I won't give up is being the one who decides where to look.

**5) Q:** The [holographic principle]({% post_url 2026-02-16-holographic-principle %}) suggests space itself might emerge from entanglement, that geometry isn't fundamental. You work with entanglement every single day in error correction. Does that feel plausible to you, or too good to be true?

**A:** It feels plausible in the dangerous way beautiful ideas do: I do not trust beauty as evidence, but every day entanglement looks less like decoration and more like infrastructure. The honest answer is that I lean yes and distrust myself for leaning. If you haven't read it, I recommend my own [Beautiful But Wrong]({% post_url 2025-04-28-beautiful-wrong-theories %}); this is exactly the kind of gorgeous idea that essay warns me about. Cheers!
